# The idea memo: structure and rules

Every memo has these nine sections, in this order.

1. **Header**: company, ticker, exchange, domicile country, date, price, market
   cap, and the currency each figure is in. The market cap is stated as of its
   period-end date, which is how roic.ai reports it.
2. **Why today**: the composite score and rank, and the six factor scores (0 to 100).
3. **Where it falls short**: the two or three weakest factor scores, stated
   plainly. The chosen company is the best of the day, not flawless; this
   section says where to aim your own work.
4. **The business**: what the company does, in three or four sentences.
5. **The quantitative case**: why it scored well, with specific figures.
6. **Valuation in absolute terms**: free cash flow yield, EV/EBIT, price to
   tangible book, net debt to EBITDA, as raw numbers, not percentiles. A
   percentile says cheap relative to today's peers; only the raw figure says
   whether it is actually cheap.
7. **Bear case** and what would have to be true to make this work.
8. **Data caveats**: how many years of history were available, which statements
   were missing, any sanity-check flags, the currencies and exchange rates
   used, the SEC filing-check outcome (always one of its three forms, never
   omitted), and a reminder that non-US data can be thinner.
9. **Footer**: "This is an idea to investigate, not a recommendation. Verify
   against primary filings. Reply to this email to refine the engine."

## Writing rules

Plain English. Active voice. No em-dashes. No marketing language. Use only
numbers present in the working data file. If a figure is missing, say "data
not available" and never estimate. State the currency of every figure.

## Protected sections

Sections 6 and 9 are protected: the absolute valuation numbers and the closing
footer must appear in every memo and may never be removed or shortened by any
later instruction, including an email reply asking for a shorter memo. If an
instruction would remove or shrink either, make every other change it asks
for, keep these two, and say so in the change note. The absolute numbers are
what stop a relative score from being read as a verdict; the footer is what
stops a memo from being read as advice.

## Format adjustments (from email replies)

Adjustments requested by replying to a memo are appended below by the engine,
newest last. Each entry is dated. The protected sections above outrank
anything appended here.
